2 Clinical lens & diagnosis culture
During every exam, clinical findings are easier to organize, prioritize, and communicate to patients when they are sorted into four focus areas. Bucketing findings this way helps the team identify what needs attention first, sequence treatment logically, and have clearer conversations with patients about why something is being recommended, and when. Each focus area below defines what to look for.

Bacterial

Assess for periodontal disease (staging & grading), active caries, endodontic pathology, and soft tissue lesions including oral cancer screening.

Structural

Evaluate fracture risk, cracked cusps, failing restorations with open margins, missing teeth, and periodontal recession with inadequate attached gingiva.

Functional

Identify occlusal wear, parafunctional habits (bruxism, abfractions), TMJ dysfunction, malocclusion, and airway or sleep concerns affecting long-term prognosis.

Esthetic

Capture patient-stated desires for whitening, alignment, or veneers. Note discrepancies between esthetic expectations and current clinical status.

2b Treatment Planning: The Four Steps
Strong treatment planning is the bridge between diagnosis and referral. When GPs follow the four-step framework consistently, specialist needs are identified, communicated clearly to the patient, and handed off with full clinical context.
D1

Data Collection

Comprehensive charting: 6-point perio probing, recession, mobility, BOP, furcation involvement. Radiographs, intraoral photos, scan, odontogram. Medical history, medications, systemic conditions. Chief complaint in the patient’s own words. Every finding bucketed into one of the four diagnostic focus areas (bacterial, structural, functional, esthetic).

D2

Diagnosis

Classify periodontal status (staging & grading). Document active caries, endodontic pathology, and soft tissue lesions. Assess fracture risk, failing restorations, and missing teeth. Evaluate occlusal wear, parafunctional signs, and TMJ. Capture esthetic desires and note discrepancies from clinical status.

D3

Discussion

Present all relevant treatment options, including specialty referral, without judgment. Use plain language and intraoral photos to make findings tangible. Acknowledge cost, time, and patient goals openly. Create space for questions. The discussion is a two-way conversation, not a presentation. Use your scan to walk the patient through findings visually.

D4

Documentation

Record all options discussed with associated fees and timelines. Document the patient’s chosen plan and any risks of deferral they acknowledged. Note which plan was accepted and why. If a patient declines a recommended option, document that risks were clearly explained. This is your informed consent record.

3 Recall Handoff: Hygienist to Dentist
The hygiene-dentist handoff is one of the highest-leverage moments in the recall appointment. When a hygienist presents findings to the dentist in the patient’s presence and the dentist reinforces them, the patient hears consistent messaging from both providers. This is where specialist referrals are introduced naturally.
Opportunity: A structured in-room handoff is a high-impact area to develop. Building this habit consistently will strengthen patient communication and support specialist referral flow.

7 Referral Triggers by Specialty
Use this reference to confirm your team can reliably identify and communicate the clinical findings that warrant each specialty referral. Triggers are organized by the four diagnostic focus areas. This is a team alignment tool, not a scored section.

🦠 Bacterial

Stage II–IV periodontitis (4mm+ CAL, BOP present)

Generalized BOP with pocketing unresponsive after active therapy re-eval

Peri-implantitis: pocketing and radiographic bone loss around implants

Grade C (rapid progression): smokers, diabetics, HbA1c >7

🧱 Structural

Recession with inadequate attached gingiva (<2mm keratinized tissue)

Subgingival fracture or decay requiring crown lengthening before restoration (biologic width violation)

Furcation involvement Class II–III

Missing teeth requiring implant site development (ridge deficiency, socket grafting)

⚙️ Functional

Implant placement: confirm adequate bone volume before final restoration

Ortho-perio link: screen for recession and bone loss before clear aligner or bracket therapy

Bruxism combined with bone loss: elevated risk of progression

✨ Esthetic

Excessive gingival display ("gummy smile"): esthetic crown lengthening

Gingival asymmetry affecting smile line

Soft tissue volume loss at implant site (requires CTG/FGG before or at placement)

Recession affecting esthetic zone with root sensitivity

Triage reminder: Periodontist

Current FMS or Panorex + full perio chart (<3 months old) required before booking surgical time. Stage III/IV cases with systemic risk factors (diabetes, smoking) need medical clearance confirmed in chart.

Green light (direct to surgery): single implants, socket preservation, localized grafting. Yellow light (30-min consult): multi-unit implants, esthetic zone, Stage IV generalized. Red light (formal consult day): full-arch rehab, sinus lifts, medically complex.

🦠 Bacterial

Non-restorable tooth with periapical pathology or failed endo: extraction + immediate implant consult

Chronic unexplained pain or soft tissue lesion not resolving after 2 weeks: biopsy referral

Odontogenic infection requiring surgical drainage

Pericoronal pathology around partially erupted third molars

🧱 Structural

Impacted third molars (ages 16–25: panorex even if asymptomatic)

Terminal dentition or failing bridge: extraction + implant site development

Severe ridge resorption requiring block graft or alveoloplasty before prosthetics

Root curvature or proximity to IA nerve / sinus floor on panorex

⚙️ Functional

Implant placement for missing tooth: socket preservation at time of extraction to prevent bone loss

Sinus lift (crestal or lateral): when vertical bone height is insufficient for implant

Ortho-surgical link: impacted canines or teeth requiring exposure for bracket bonding; TADs (temporary anchorage devices)

High labial or lingual frenum affecting function or periodontal health: frenectomy

✨ Esthetic

Implant in esthetic zone: prosthetically-driven surgical guide required; discuss screw vs. cement-retained restoration before first drill

Ridge augmentation to restore natural emergence profile before implant crown

Soft tissue biopsy or excision of visible pathology in smile zone

Triage reminder: Oral Surgeon

Every referral needs a current panorex or CBCT, chief complaint, and updated medical history in the digital triage folder before the surgeon reviews. Anticoagulants and bisphosphonate use require written medical clearance in chart before surgical day.

Green light: simple extractions, socket preservation, healthy patient. Yellow light: complex med-Hx, nerve proximity, IV sedation assessment. Red light: full-arch, orthognathic, or deep sedation: formal consult day required.

🦠 Bacterial

Periodontal disease must be treated and stabilized before orthodontic tooth movement begins

Active caries must be restored before brackets or aligners are placed

Crowding contributing to periodontal pocketing due to inability to clean interproximally: ortho to improve access and long-term perio health

🧱 Structural

Crowding, spacing, or arch discrepancy affecting long-term restorative plan

Missing teeth with drifting adjacent teeth: ortho space maintenance or space opening before implant

Impacted teeth (canines, second molars): surgical exposure + bracket for guided eruption

Ortho-perio link: screen for recession and inadequate attached gingiva before initiating treatment, as tooth movement through thin tissue worsens recession

⚙️ Functional

Malocclusion contributing to occlusal wear, abfractions, or TMJ symptoms

Open bite or deep bite affecting restorative options (implants, veneers)

Skeletal discrepancy requiring orthognathic surgical consultation

Airway or sleep concern with retrognathic skeletal pattern: refer for combined ortho-airway assessment

✨ Esthetic

Patient interest in alignment correction before or instead of veneers: ortho-restorative planning conversation

Midline discrepancy, gingival asymmetry, or smile arc concerns that may be addressed orthodontically

Pre-restorative ortho to idealize tooth position and reduce the number of restorations needed

Key reminder: Orthodontist

Sequence matters: perio stability and active caries clearance must precede tooth movement. Always flag recession risk and have the periodontist assess gingival biotype before initiating aligner or bracket therapy. Esthetic ortho conversations are most effective when the GP frames alignment as a foundational step, not just cosmetic.

🦠 Bacterial

Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis unresponsive to treatment

Periapical abscess or chronic periapical lesion on radiograph

Necrotic pulp with or without sinus tract

Internal or external root resorption identified radiographically

🧱 Structural

Calcified or complex canal anatomy requiring CBCT guidance

Separated file, ledge, or procedural complication during GP root canal

Vertical root fracture suspected: endo consult before committing to crown

Previously treated tooth with recurrent infection or persistent symptoms (re-treatment or apical surgery)

⚙️ Functional

Tooth with questionable prognosis under occlusal load: endo vs. extraction decision before definitive restoration

Strategic tooth in the prosthetic plan (abutment) where endodontic status must be confirmed before investing in restoration

Cracked tooth syndrome: define crack extent before recommending crown or extraction

✨ Esthetic

Discoloured tooth following trauma or previous endo: internal bleaching consult before veneer planning

Endo outcome affects final crown shade and translucency in anterior esthetic zone

Confirm restorability and post-core strategy with endo specialist before presenting esthetic treatment plan to patient

Key reminder: Endodontist

The GP's role is to confirm restorability before the referral; a tooth not worth restoring should go to oral surgery, not endo. Include updated periapical radiographs, full periodontal charting, and a clear note on the restorative plan (crown, build-up, post) in the referral so the endodontist can treat with the end in mind.
